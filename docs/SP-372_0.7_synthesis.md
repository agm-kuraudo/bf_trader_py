> **Status:** complete. The 0.7 release review for epic **SP-367** (Betfair Data

## The 0.7 question, answered

0.7 asked one thing:

> **Is there anything tradeable in the captured Betfair odds data?**

**Answer: not with the two odds-only strategies tested, on the data as currently
captured.** Both candidate edges were assessed honestly and **neither survives
costs**: cross-outcome green-out arbitrage is rare and tiny (SP-370), and
pre-match drift trading loses because the drift is smaller than the spread
(SP-371). This is a *successful* 0.7 outcome in the terms the release set itself
("a perfectly good 0.7 outcome is 'no clear edge found, here's the evidence'") —
the dataset is now clean, understood, and the two obvious strategies are ruled
out with evidence rather than hunch.

Crucially, the work also surfaced **what would have to change** to ask better
questions (coherent capture + volume/size), captured as a dependency-linked set
of follow-ups (SP-396/384/397/398/399).

## What each piece found

### SP-369 — Exploratory characterisation
*(`docs/SP-369_odds_behaviour_analysis.md`)*

- Dataset is clean and usable: **25,657 records, 0 unparseable, 41 matches**;
  implied-probability book computable for 99.7% of instants.
- **Headline signal: favourites drift *out* (lengthen) pre-match, +2.73% on
  average.** The field as a whole is ~symmetric around zero. This was the most
  promising-looking pattern and fed directly into SP-371.
- Pre-match book sits just above 1.0 (tight ~0.6% overround); in-play is
  volatile and event-driven (book 0.31–1.80).
- **No matched-volume data** — null for all rows (SP-384). 0.7 is price-only.

### SP-370 — Green-out / cross-outcome arbitrage
*(`docs/SP-370_green_out_scan.md`)* — **verdict: NOT VIABLE**

- A priced back-all-three green-out (book < 1.0 on best-back) does occur, but
  **pre-match it is rare (3.3%) and tiny (median 0.13% after commission)**.
- In-play "green-outs" (up to 207% "profit") are **forward-fill artifacts**, not
  real opportunities — a consequence of the timestamp mismatch (see below).
- No liquidity data, so even the small pre-match flags can't be confirmed as
  matchable. Expected result for a single liquid exchange; now proven.

### SP-371 — Pre-match drift trading
*(`docs/SP-371_drift_trading.md`)* — **verdict: NOT VIABLE**

- Despite SP-369's real +2.73% drift signal, trading it **loses ~1–2% per unit**
  in every framing (momentum + mean-reversion) and every entry/exit window.
- **Why:** the drift is smaller than the **back/lay spread** you cross on a round
  trip (median pre-match spread **1.33%**). A directional tendency that doesn't
  beat transaction costs.
- Best-price fills (optimistic); real fills only make it worse. Delivered as
  "option C" with size-aware fills descoped to SP-399.

## Cross-cutting lessons (worth more than the two verdicts)

1. **The capture shape limits the questions, not just the answers.** The three
   analyses kept hitting the same two walls:
   - **No volume / available-size** (SP-384) — can't judge liquidity, so a priced
     edge can't be confirmed as a *matchable* edge.
   - **Runners captured at distinct timestamps** (SP-396) — forces an as-of
     forward-fill that is benign pre-match but fabricates impossible in-play
     books, making in-play cross-outcome analysis unmeasurable today.
   These aren't bugs in the analysis or the SP-368 loader — they're capture
   limitations, and fixing them is the single highest-leverage thing for any
   future Betfair strategy work.
2. **A real signal isn't a real edge.** SP-369 found a genuine, statistically
   visible drift; SP-371 showed it's untradeable after the spread. The gap
   between "a pattern exists" and "you can profit from it after costs" is the
   whole game, and 0.7 measured it properly.
3. **The tooling is reusable and trustworthy.** The pure-core / thin-wrapper
   split (SP-368 loader → `odds_behaviour` / `green_out` / `drift_trading`) means
   every verdict is property-tested and every scan re-runs in seconds against a
   fresh parquet. 0.8+ builds on this, not from scratch.

## Recommendation for 0.8

0.8's established theme is **BACKTEST candidate strategies properly**. But 0.7
found **no candidate worth a full backtest** — both odds-only strategies are
ruled out, and backtesting a known-losing strategy adds nothing. So the honest
0.8 recommendation is a **change of tack**, in priority order:

1. **Fix capture first — SP-396 (coherent per-poll snapshot) + SP-384 (volume /
   available-size).** This is the prerequisite for *every* better question. Both
   are small, well-scoped, and unlock the three deferred re-assessments. **Do
   these before any further analysis.** Highest priority.
2. **Decide whether Betfair match-odds is the right market at all.** 0.7's
   evidence is that EPL/UCL MATCH_ODDS is an efficient, tight-spread market where
   the obvious crude edges don't exist. Before investing more, consider whether a
   different market (less liquid competitions, other sports, in-play with proper
   capture) is more promising — or whether the effort is better spent elsewhere.
   This is a strategic call, not a code task.
3. **If continuing on Betfair, pursue the outcome-based direction via a results
   feed.** The one strategy family 0.7 *couldn't* test is drift-as-winner-signal
   held to settlement (drift *sense 2*) — it needs match results, which 0.7
   deliberately excluded. This is the natural "does the drift actually predict
   winners?" question and would pull in the **deferred results-sourcing spike**.
   Only worth it if #2 says Betfair is still the target.
4. **Re-run the ruled-out analyses only after capture is fixed** — SP-397
   (pre-match green-out), SP-398 (in-play green-out), SP-399 (size-aware drift).
   Low priority: expected to confirm the 0.7 verdicts, so they add rigour, not a
   new edge. Pull in at refinement if/when capacity allows.

### In one line

> **0.8 should fix capture (SP-396 + SP-384) and make a strategic market
> decision — not backtest, because 0.7 found nothing worth backtesting. The data
> is efficient; the next edge (if any) needs either better data or a different
> market.**
