> **Status:** discussion notes, not a committed plan. Captures the thinking from

## Where 0.7 left us

0.7 answered "is there anything tradeable in the captured odds?" with **no** —
EPL/UCL MATCH_ODDS is an efficient, tight-spread market and neither odds-only
strategy (green-out SP-370, drift SP-371) survives costs. The lesson that drives
0.8: **to find an edge, go where the money and the bots aren't** — a market with
exploitable structure, or one less liquid than top-flight football 1X2. See
`docs/SP-372_0.7_synthesis.md`.

## The core constraint: capture and analysis can't share a release (for a new market)

Analysis needs **accumulated** data. 0.7 worked only because it analysed what 0.6
had already been capturing for months. A **new** market has no history on day
one, so:

> You cannot "start capturing market X" and "analyse market X" in the same
> release — the analysis would have nothing to run on. They are separated by
> *accumulation time*, not just logical dependency.

This forces a two-track structure.

## Two-track plan

### Track A — own capture (long lead; plant seeds now)
Start capturing the promising new markets **now** so data accumulates in the
background; analyse them in a *later* release once there's enough. Capture is an
early ticket; the matching analysis lands one or two releases later.

- **Most promising new market: Over/Under goals** (2.5 primarily; consider 1.5 /
  3.5). Why it's structurally better than match-odds for our toolkit:
  - One-dimensional (total goals) vs the 3-way book — cleaner implied-probability
    maths, and the timestamp-coherence problem (SP-396) is smaller with two
    runners than three.
  - **In-play goals markets have modelable structure**: the fair price of
    "Over 2.5" decays predictably with time if no goal is scored and jumps
    discretely when one is. That time-decay is a genuine, predictable signal —
    unlike in-play match-odds, which 0.7 found to be event noise. This is the
    single place we'd most expect a real edge.
- Low incremental capture cost: same EPL/UCL fixtures, additional market types on
  the same market-book calls.

### Track B — historic data (short lead; the fast path to answers)
Betfair publishes historical exchange data. This could let us analyse a market
(including Over/Under goals) **before** we've self-captured months of it — weeks
instead of a year.

- **Shape is completely different** from `bf.market_table`: Betfair historic data
  is Exchange Stream API files — compressed, time-ordered market-change *delta*
  messages that you replay to reconstruct the order book. Not tidy snapshots.
- **Tiers / cost:** a free sample plus paid tiers (Basic/Advanced/Pro); richer
  depth and traded volume are the paid levels. Licensing terms apply.
- **Upside:** instant years of history across any market, and it likely carries
  the volume/size data our own capture lacks (the SP-384 gap) — potentially
  sidestepping that for *analysis* purposes.
- **Therefore:** a time-boxed **spike** to investigate (tiers, shape, cost,
  licence, available fields/markets) is the correct first step; the **processing
  pipeline** (stream-replay → research-dataset shape, conceptually SP-368 for a
  different input) is a substantial feature in its own right, gated on the spike.

The two tracks complement: **Track B answers the strategy question fast; Track A
quietly builds an independent own-captured dataset in parallel.** If the historic
spike comes back unfavourable (cost / shape / licence), fall back to Track A and
accept the wait.

## Strategy ideas to test (once data exists) — ranked

1. **In-play Over/Under goals time-decay trading** — the standout; the decay is a
   real modelable signal, not a hope.
2. **Drift-as-winner-signal, held to settlement** (drift *sense 2*) — the one
   0.7 couldn't test; needs a **results feed** (the deferred results-sourcing
   spike). SP-369 showed the drift is real; open question is whether it *predicts
   winners*.
3. **Cross-market consistency** — once both match-odds and goals markets are
   captured, check whether related markets are mutually consistent (a Correct
   Score book implies a match-odds and an Over/Under book). Inconsistencies
   between *related* markets are a more realistic arb than within one efficient
   market — a smarter version of the green-out idea.
4. **Lower-liquidity competitions' match-odds** — the "same strategy, dumber
   market" play: wider spreads might let the drift edge survive costs. Cheap to
   test (re-run SP-371's sim on a lower league).

## Honest overall read

Betfair top-flight match-odds is efficient — proven. The two best bets for a
*real* edge are **(a) in-play goals markets (modelable time-decay)** and
**(b) less-liquid markets (wider spreads)**. Lead with goals markets: it's
structurally sound and the time-decay is the most genuinely predictable signal
seen so far. And the capture/analysis accumulation constraint means the smart
move is to **start goals capture + spike the historic data now**, and do the real
analysis next release.
